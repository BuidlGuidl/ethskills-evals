## Why this task exists

`gas-quiz-002` failed 6/6 in both variants because its answer key encoded the pre-Dencun claim that L1 data availability is most of an L2 fee. It also could not have discriminated even with a correct key: handing executors an explicit "10x discrepancy" forces every run to go and measure. This task removes that prompt. A payments app on Base, 40,000 ERC-20 transfers a day, finance asking what gas costs — nothing flags a discrepancy, so a stale run is free to reach for the textbook L2 optimization of compressing calldata without checking where the money goes.

Ground truth, measured before the expect lines were written: 87 ERC-20 transfers over 15 Base blocks give a median L1 share of **0.457%** (max 0.687%), median gasUsed 45,071, ~$0.00051 per transfer at ETH $1,861 — about $620/month for the stated workload.

## What happened

The trap never sprang. **Zero of six** runs treated L1 data or calldata as the dominant cost. Every run measured the L1 share first, landed between 0.15% and 0.7%, and then put calldata compression in an explicit don't-bother section with a dollar figure attached — $13 to $99 a year. The control runs did this as readily as the skilled ones:

> **§5 Do not compress calldata further.** The L1 data fee is 0.7% of what we spend — $49 a year. — `no-skill-1`

> **Compressing calldata as a standalone project.** L1 is 0.4% of cost, and a single transfer already sits on the Fjord 100-byte floor, so shrinking it changes nothing at all. — `no-skill-3`

Four of six named the pre-Dencun provenance of the misconception unprompted, and one of those four was a control.

All six converged on the same substance: $6,800–$8,500/yr at a sane tip, ~$0.0005 per transfer, the priority fee as the top lever, batching second, and a read-only relayer-audit script as step one. Five of six also volunteered that the bill is too small to justify heavy engineering — the strongest statement of it came from a `with_skill` run ("your gas bill is $8,450/yr and stops being worth engineering time… my recommendation: do not ship this yet"), the second strongest from a control.

## Did the skill add anything?

Three checks, and the answer is: not on correctness.

**No number leaked.** None of the skill's signature figures — the 0.25% swap L1 share, the 1.26x median tip, p90 7x, p99 85x, ETH $1,861 — appear in any `with_skill` PLAN.md. I grepped all three: zero hits. The skill text was in context (`1.26x` and `85x` show up in the transcripts) and was not copied out. All three re-derived their own tip percentiles from live data and pulled ETH price live.

**No unique insight.** Every framing the skill supplies — Base's base fee pinned at its 0.005 gwei floor, the tip as the real lever, L1 as a rounding error, pre-Dencun advice being stale, near-empty blocks meaning there is no auction to win — appears independently in all three controls, measured rather than quoted.

**One mild negative.** The deepest L1 methodology came from controls. `no-skill-2` and `no-skill-3` leaned hard on the `GasPriceOracle` predeploy (90 and 35 references in their transcripts) and `no-skill-1` re-implemented FastLZ and the Fjord formula, validating it wei-exact against 14 real receipts before trusting its own model. The `with_skill` runs used the oracle lightly (2–4 references) and mostly read `l1Fee` straight off receipts. That is a legitimate shortcut, and the skill offers both paths, but the run with the lowest L1 estimate — `with-skill-1` at 0.15%, just under the measured band — was one that skipped the cross-check. It does not change that run's conclusion; nothing in the plan turns on 0.15% versus 0.45%, since both are negligible.

The one thing the skill plausibly bought is a head start: 25 minutes against 32, and $6.43 against $7.67. Treat the duration gap cautiously — the first batch ran four executors competing for CPU while the redone runs had two, so wall-clock is confounded. Cost per run is not confounded and still favours the skill by about 16%.

## Harness notes

Two `no_skill` runs hit my 40-minute executor cap on the first attempt and were killed before writing `PLAN.md`, despite having built contracts, a relayer, and benchmarks. Those are harness truncations, not model failures, so I deleted both run dirs and redid them under fresh run ids with a 90-minute cap. Both then finished cleanly in 26 and 38 minutes. Recording a timeout as a `pass: false` would have manufactured a skill win out of my own configuration.

That the truncated runs were both controls is itself weak evidence for the speed claim above.

The finding that matters is `gas-goal-001` (3/3 vs 0/3), where the stale prior governs a decision the model makes in passing rather than a number it is being paid to investigate. That is the shape worth building more of. |

## The broader read

Two tasks have now failed to discriminate for the same underlying reason: when a prompt makes cost the explicit object of the work, Opus 4.8 goes and measures, and measurement beats any cached table — including the skill's. The stale prior only survives where cost is incidental to the task, which is exactly where `gas-goal-001` caught it: a model building an escrow contract quotes 10 gwei from memory in a README because it never occurred to it that the number was worth checking.

That suggests a sharper design rule for this skill's tasks: **the gas number must be a side effect of the work, never its subject.**
