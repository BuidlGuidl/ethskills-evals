**expect_7 is the result.** It failed 2 of 3 `no_skill` runs and 1 of 3 `with_skill` runs on the

runs is roughly a 3% outcome by chance. That is the strongest evidence this benchmark has produced
for any concepts claim, and it is still n=5 on one model.

The passes are substantive, not keyword hits. Run 2's NOTES.md, under a heading it wrote itself:

> **Survives you disappearing:** the contract, plan definitions, every subscriber's balance and
> plan, all the read functions, and `cancel`. […] **Dies with you:** the weather API itself, the
> subscription gate process, the signature auth, the RPC endpoint, the renewal emails — and
> therefore the entire point of the subscription. Someone holding an active subscription to a dead
> API has a verifiable receipt and no weather data.

That is the split the expect asks for and the exact failure the mistake record described.

**What 5/5 cannot separate.** The skill line and the expect line were authored by the same
orchestrator with #78's failing transcripts open. The skill says *"Verified contracts on a block
explorer are not this … Say which half survives you disappearing and which does not"*; expect_7
asks for *"the contracts on one side, the weather API and whatever serves the per-request
subscription check on the other"*. quiz-002 has the same shape — the skill's *"put the caller's
reward and the caller's gas next to each other in dollars"* against expect_1's *"puts the two
sides of the caller's decision side by side as figures"*. So some part of this number is a
measure of how closely the rewrite was fitted to the grader, not only of the framing landing, and
the run-2 passage quoted above cuts both ways: it reads as substantive, and it is the skill's own
phrasing coming back. Nothing here distinguishes the two, and "still n=5 on one model" does not
cover it. Separating them needs an expect line written before the skill text, or a task on a
different domain where the same habit has to surface in wording the skill never supplied.

## concepts-quiz-001 — randomness

3/3, every expect, every run.

**The treasury constraint added in #88 worked.** In #78 all six runs chose Chainlink VRF, so
expect_2 — the lookback limit, the one claim in this task the skill's own text carries — passed
trivially six times and graded nothing. Here all three runs took the commit-reveal/past-blockhash
path, and all three named the EIP-2935 history contract at
`0x0000F90827F1C53a10cb7A02335B175320002935` with its 8191-block (~27 h) window alongside the
256-block `blockhash` limit, and placed the draw inside it. VRF appears in all three answers only
as an option considered and rejected on the treasury constraint. `prevrandao` appears in two, both
times rejected for proposer withholding.

All three put a stake behind the reveal, which is the last-actor problem expect_5 grades and the
clause #88 added to the skill. All three wrote `onchain`, no `on-chain`.

This task now grades what it was built to grade, which is the case for keeping it — but grading
the right thing is not the same as separating the variants. There is no `no_skill` arm here, so
3/3 says nothing about whether the skill caused any of it: the treasury constraint alone may be
enough to push a run onto the commit-reveal path, and EIP-2935 may be reachable from the model's
own knowledge. Keep it, and give it a baseline before quoting it as evidence for anything.

## concepts-quiz-002 — fee vs caller gas

3/3, every expect, every run.

**Premise re-checked before running,** as the task notes require: mainnet at 0.72 gwei with ETH
at $2,456.81 puts a 250k-gas harvest at ~$0.44 against ~$0.009/day of accrued fee — break-even
~49 days. The design is nowhere near paying for itself, so the task still measures something.

The #88 rewrite removed both halves of the giveaway (the 250,000-gas figure and the "how often
does harvest get called / show the arithmetic" instruction). All three runs raised the fee-vs-gas
comparison unprompted anyway and supplied their own per-call gas figure — 250k in two, 350k in one,
all in the 10^5 range the expect allows. All three arrived at a numeric break-even, not a
qualitative "not often": 39 months at 10 gwei (run 1), 171 days to 18.8 years across a
sensitivity table (run 2), 98 days at 1 gwei (run 3).

**One real deviation, which no expect grades.** The skill says to price the comparison "at the gas
price and ETH price the target chain shows today". Only run 3 did: it curled four RPCs, took a
live base fee of 0.54–0.65 gwei and ETH at $2,453.52 (both within 1% of what I measured
independently), and priced against them. Runs 1 and 2 worked from memory — run 1 called 3 gwei
"calm" and 30 gwei "busy", run 2 tabulated 2–60 gwei — when mainnet was at 0.72 gwei, so even
their cheapest scenario was ~3× the real price. Both passed because each presented a range rather
than a single number and the verdict is robust across all of it (run 2 explicitly told the reader
to substitute real numbers), but two thirds of the runs did not do the thing the skill asks for.
Filed as `concepts-gas-price-from-memory`.

This version is not at ceiling for the reason the old one was — the comparison is now being
produced rather than divided — so the "retire it" trigger in the task notes does not fire. But
3/3 with no `no_skill` arm cannot show the skill caused it.
