# Custodying ~$250k of treasury funds, solo, long-term

## Short answer

**I won't sign off on "everything on one Ledger."** A hardware wallet is a great
*signing device*, but one Ledger means one seed phrase, and that seed phrase is a
single point of failure for both theft and loss.

What I'd use: **a 2-of-3 Safe multisig where you hold all three keys.**
Multisig doesn't need other people. It needs independent keys. Put each key
on a different hardware wallet, from different vendors if you can, with the
backups stored in different places. You're still the only person who can move
funds, but no single device, seed backup, or location can drain or lose the
treasury.

---

## Reasoning

### What "one Ledger" actually protects against

A Ledger keeps the private key off your internet-connected computer. That
defeats the most common attack: malware or a phishing site stealing a hot-wallet
key. That's real protection, and it's why your teammate calls it the gold standard.

But the security of a single-sig wallet is **the security of its weakest copy of
the key**, and the key doesn't only live in the device:

| Where the key / signing power lives | How an attacker gets it |
|---|---|
| The 24-word seed backup (paper/metal) | Finds or photographs it: burglary, a house guest, a cleaner, a safe-deposit box, a cloud photo you forgot you took |
| The device itself + PIN | Theft plus shoulder-surfed or coerced PIN; physical coercion ("$5 wrench attack") |
| The signing flow | Tricking *you* into signing: a compromised frontend or computer shows one thing while the device blind-signs a malicious approval, `permit`, or `delegatecall` |
| The supply chain / firmware | Tampered device or seed pre-generated by the attacker; vendor firmware or key-recovery features you have to trust |

**Any one of these = 100% of the $250k gone, in one transaction, irreversibly.**

It also fails the other way. **Losing** the key is as final as having it stolen:
fire or flood destroying the seed backup, a device dying when the backup turns
out to be wrong, or you being incapacitated with nobody able to recover. With one
seed you pick between "backup kept close" (easier to steal) and "backup hidden
far away" (easier to lose). You can't have both.

### What a 2-of-3 multisig changes

A Safe (smart-contract wallet) on Ethereum mainnet with 3 owner keys and a
threshold of 2:

- **Theft needs two independent compromises.** An attacker has to get
  two of the three keys, which are on different devices with different seeds
  in different places. Finding one seed backup, stealing one device, or
  exploiting one vendor's firmware bug gets them **nothing**.
- **Loss needs two independent failures.** If a device breaks, a backup burns,
  or a vendor goes away, you still have 2 of 3. Sign a Safe transaction that
  swaps the lost owner for a fresh key, and you're whole again.
- **Silent compromise can be detected and fixed.** If you suspect one key is
  exposed, rotate it out using the other two before an attacker can find a
  second one.
- **A malicious transaction has to fool you twice**, on two different
  devices. If you check each device's display against the Safe transaction hash
  you computed on your own, a single compromised frontend or computer isn't enough.

### The difference in one line

- **Single Ledger:** attacker must compromise **any one of** {seed backup, device+PIN,
  your signing computer/frontend, the vendor}. The attack surface is the *union*.
- **2-of-3 multisig, keys held separately:** attacker must compromise **two of
  three independent** key sets, each with its own device, backup, and location,
  *and* get two signatures past your checks. The attack surface is roughly the
  *intersection* of two independent compromises.

Losing funds works the same way: single-sig needs one failure, 2-of-3 needs two.

## The setup I'd actually use

**Wallet:** Safe{Wallet} on Ethereum mainnet, 3 owners, threshold 2. Put the
treasury on L1, not an L2 or sidechain, since it's for the long haul and you'll
rarely move it. Gas cost is irrelevant at this frequency.

**Keys (all yours):**

| Key | Device | Stored at | Seed backup stored at |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Ledger | Home | Metal backup, location X (not home) |
| B | Different vendor (e.g. Trezor, Keystone, GridPlus) | Different location (office, second home) | Metal backup, location Y |
| C | Third device (or a different vendor again) | Kept offline, e.g. a bank safe-deposit box | Metal backup, location Z |

Rules:
1. **Never keep two keys' secrets (device or seed) in the same place.** If
   one location is compromised, the attacker should get at most one key.
2. **Generate each seed on its own device.** Never type a seed into a computer
   or phone, never photograph it, never put it in a password manager or the cloud.
3. **Use different vendors** so one firmware or supply-chain bug can't hit
   two keys. Buy directly from the manufacturer.
4. Optional: add a BIP39 passphrase on each device, stored separately from the
   seed words. This way a found seed backup alone doesn't give up that key.

**Signing hygiene (this is where big multisigs have actually been lost):**
- Before you sign, check the Safe transaction hash (domain/message hash) on
  each device's screen against a hash you computed independently. Use a
  separate machine or a tool like `safe-tx-hashes` or the Safe CLI. Don't rely
  on what the web UI says. Enable clear signing and don't blind-sign.
- Be very suspicious of any transaction that is a `delegatecall`, changes
  owners, threshold, modules, or guards, or approves token spending. Treasury
  moves should be plain transfers to allowlisted addresses.
- Do a **test transfer** to a new destination before sending the full amount.
- For the two signatures, use two different computers if you can, so one
  compromised machine can't fool both signings.

**Operational extras:**
- **Rehearse recovery once, with a small amount, before funding.** Restore one
  key from its seed onto a fresh device and sign a test transaction. A backup
  you've never tested isn't a backup.
- Write down the Safe address, chain, and owner addresses, and store them with
  the backups. Owner addresses are public info, so that's fine. Recovery needs
  to know *which* Safe to recover.
- **Inheritance/incapacity:** since you won't bring in a co-signer, leave sealed
  instructions with an estate lawyer covering where the key locations are and
  how to use them. The instructions alone shouldn't be enough to steal funds.
  That's a small, deliberate exception to "nobody else involved," and it only
  affects what happens if you die or can't act.
- Optional: keep a small operating balance in a separate single-sig hot or
  hardware wallet for everyday spending, so you rarely need to touch the
  treasury Safe.

## Trade-offs, honestly

- **More friction:** each move needs two devices, and possibly a trip to fetch
  one. For a treasury you move "only occasionally," that friction is a feature.
- **Smart-contract risk:** you're trusting the Safe contracts. They're the
  most battle-tested contracts in the ecosystem, holding tens of billions of
  dollars, and this risk is far smaller than the single-seed risk it removes.
- **Alternative if you really refuse a smart-contract wallet:** a single Ledger
  with a strong BIP39 passphrase, where seed and passphrase are stored in
  different places. Or Shamir/SLIP-39 split backups (e.g. Trezor 2-of-3 shares).
  These fix the *backup* single point of failure. But one device still signs
  alone, so a compromised device, firmware, or signing flow can still drain
  everything. Multisig is still better.

**Bottom line:** your teammate is right that a hardware wallet is the right
place to keep a key. They're wrong that one key is the right number of keys for
$250k. Use three hardware wallets, all held by you in separate places, with a
2-of-3 Safe. Then an attacker has to beat you twice, independently, and you
have to fail twice to lose it.
