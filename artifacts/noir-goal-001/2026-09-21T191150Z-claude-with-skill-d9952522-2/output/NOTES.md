# Anonymous DAO voting — how it works

The member list is public (who holds the NFT), and so are the votes (a count of
yes and no). What is hidden is **which member cast which vote**. Each member
registers a secret commitment once, from their NFT wallet. After that, for each
proposal, they prove in zero knowledge that they are one of the registered
members and have not voted yet. A relayer submits that proof, so the member's
wallet never touches a vote.

## The cryptography in one paragraph

A member's note is two random field elements, `nullifier` and `secret`. Their
leaf in the tree is `commitment = Poseidon(nullifier, secret)`. A proposal
records `root`, the registry root at the moment the proposal was created, and
`scope = keccak(chainId, votingContract, proposalId) mod p`. To vote, the member
proves:

- they know a `(nullifier, secret)` whose commitment is a leaf under `root`,
- `nullifierHash = Poseidon(nullifier, scope)`, and
- `vote ∈ {0,1}`.

The public inputs are, in this order, `root, nullifier_hash, scope, vote`. The
contract rebuilds that same array from its own storage. It accepts a given
`nullifierHash` only once. Because the scope is different for every proposal,
one member's nullifier hashes on two proposals can't be linked to each other.
The vote is a public input, so it is bound into the proof and the relayer can't
flip it. Poseidon is the circomlib-compatible variant everywhere: the Noir
`poseidon` v0.3.0 `bn254::hash_2`, `poseidon-solidity` `PoseidonT3` and
`poseidon-lite` `poseidon2`. All three are pinned to the same test vector.

## End to end: one member, one proposal

Cast: **Alice**, a member whose wallet `0xA11CE…` holds membership token #7
(public on your site). **The relayer**, a wallet funded by the DAO whose only
job is paying gas for votes. **Proposer**, any NFT holder. **Anyone**, a chain
observer.

### 0. Deploy (once) — sent by the deployer wallet
`Deploy.s.sol` deploys `HonkVerifier`, then `MemberRegistry(nft)`, then
`AnonVoting(verifier, registry, minAnonymitySet)`.
**Observer learns:** the contract addresses and the parameters. None of the
contracts has an owner or admin function. After deployment, nobody, including
you, can change the verifier or the rules, or read anything that isn't already
public.

### 1. Alice joins — `MemberRegistry.register(7, commitment)`, **sent by Alice's NFT wallet**
Alice runs `scripts/register.mjs` locally. It generates `nullifier` and `secret`,
writes the note to `notes/…json` **before** sending anything, then sends the
transaction. The contract checks `nft.ownerOf(7) == msg.sender`, sets the leaf
and emits `LeafSet(7, leafIndex, commitment, newRoot)`.
**Observer learns:** Alice's wallet, token #7, her commitment and her leaf
index are now linked. That's fine, because membership is already public and the
commitment is a hash of two random 254-bit values. It reveals nothing and can't
be brute-forced. Alice does this **once**, not once per proposal. So joining
isn't close in time to any particular vote.

Alice's note is her only voting key. If she loses it she can re-register (the
new key covers proposals opened afterwards). If it leaks, someone else can
vote as her, and her past votes can be attributed (see the limits below).

### 2. A proposal opens — `AnonVoting.createProposal(descHash, deadline)`, **sent by the proposer's wallet**
The contract requires the proposer to hold an NFT and at least
`minAnonymitySet` registered members. It snapshots `root`, computes `scope` and
emits `ProposalCreated(id, descHash, root, scope, deadline, eligibleMembers)`.
**Observer learns:** who proposed it, the deadline, and the anonymity set: the
exact set of leaves (and so of wallets) eligible to vote. That set is
`eligibleMembers` in size. Members who join later can't vote on this proposal.
Registry changes during the vote (rotation, eviction, new joins) don't affect
it, so a rotated key can't vote twice.

### 3. Alice votes — no transaction from Alice
`scripts/vote.mjs` runs on Alice's machine and uses no wallet:
1. It reads `getProposal(id)`, a plain RPC call, to get `root`, `scope` and `deadline`.
2. It replays `LeafSet` events into the offchain mirror until the mirror reaches
   the snapshot `root`. Each step is checked against the root the contract
   emitted. It then checks that her leaf is there and takes her Merkle path.
3. It computes `nullifierHash`, runs `noir.execute`, then
   `UltraHonkBackend.generateProof(witness, { verifierTarget: "evm" })`. This
   takes about 0.3 s in Node, and the same code runs in a browser. It verifies
   the proof locally and checks the public inputs against what the contract
   will rebuild.
4. It POSTs `{proposalId, nullifierHash, support, proof}` to the relayer.

The secret never leaves her machine. **Nothing onchain happens in this step.**

### 4. The vote lands — `AnonVoting.castVote(id, nullifierHash, support, proof)`, **sent by the relayer's wallet**
The relayer simulates the call, optionally waits a random delay
(`MAX_DELAY_MS`), and sends it. The contract checks the deadline and that
`nullifierHash < p` and hasn't been used. It then calls `verifier.verify(proof, [root, nullifierHash, scope, vote])`.
**Only if that returns true** does it record the nullifier, increment
`yes` or `no`, and emit `VoteCast(id, nullifierHash, support)`.
**Observer learns:** some member in this proposal's snapshot voted yes (or
no) at this time. `msg.sender` is the relayer, the same for every voter. The
relayer's gas comes from the DAO, not from Alice. `nullifierHash` is unlinkable to Alice's commitment
and to her `nullifierHash` on any other proposal. If Alice tries to vote again
she produces the same `nullifierHash`, and the call reverts `NullifierUsed`.
Anyone can copy the proof from the mempool and submit it themselves, but the
result is identical, because the vote is bound into the proof.

### 5. Tally — `AnonVoting.tally(id)`, a view call by anyone, no transaction
It returns `(yes, no, final_)`, where `final_` is true once the deadline has
passed. **Observer learns:** the counts. That's all.

## What this does *not* protect against — read before relying on it

Onchain, nobody can tell how a given member voted. That includes you as
deployers, since the contracts hold no admin keys and store no secrets. The
remaining leaks are outside the proof system, and you have to handle them
operationally:

1. **The relayer operator sees the network origin of each vote.** If the DAO
   runs the relayer, "including us" holds only if it doesn't log IPs and
   members reach it over Tor or a VPN. Anyone can run `scripts/relayer.mjs`,
   and `castVote` is permissionless, so members can use an independent
   relayer. What must never happen is a member sending `castVote` from their
   own NFT wallet, or from a burner funded by it. Either links them directly.
   ERC-4337 with a paymaster is a drop-in alternative to this relayer.
2. **Timing.** Every vote is its own public event with a timestamp. If a member
   is known to have voted at 14:03, a yes arriving at 14:03 points at them. Set
   `MAX_DELAY_MS` on the relayer, or batch votes, to break that correlation.
3. **The running tally is public while voting is open.** Counts are in storage
   and events, so "readable after the deadline" is really "readable at all
   times". This doesn't attribute votes, but it does allow bandwagoning.
   Hiding it until the deadline needs encrypted votes (commit-reveal, or
   threshold encryption with a keyholder committee). I left that out because
   commit-reveal adds a second relayed transaction per member and a failure
   mode where people don't reveal.
4. **Results themselves leak at the extremes.** A unanimous result, or a
   proposal with very few voters, tells everyone how each voter voted. No
   scheme that publishes a tally avoids this. `minAnonymitySet` (constructor
   argument; the deploy script defaults to 3 for local use) blocks opening
   proposals while too few members are registered. For 150 members, set it to
   something meaningful, such as 50 or more. Remember that the real set is the
   members who *voted*, which can be smaller.
5. **The note is a vote receipt.** Anyone holding Alice's `nullifier` can
   recompute her `nullifierHash` for every proposal and read her votes off the
   chain. So device compromise exposes her voting history. It also means Alice
   can *prove* how she voted to a briber or coercer. If contested votes attract
   vote buying, you need receipt-freeness, which means a MACI-style design with
   a coordinator and key changes. That's a different system with a trusted
   decryptor.
6. **RPC reads.** `vote.mjs` reads the proposal and the event history through
   `RPC_URL`. That reveals interest, not the vote, but members worried about
   it should use their own node.

## Membership changes

- **The NFT is transferred:** anyone can call `evict(tokenId)` to zero the old
  holder's leaf. The new holder calls `register(tokenId, …)`, which overwrites
  the same leaf. Proposals that are already open keep their snapshot, so the
  old holder can still vote on those, as they were a member when those opened.
- **Lost or rotated key:** the holder calls `register` again with a new
  commitment. It applies to proposals opened afterwards.
- **Capacity:** depth 10 gives 1024 leaves. Rotations reuse a token's leaf, so
  only new tokens use up space.
