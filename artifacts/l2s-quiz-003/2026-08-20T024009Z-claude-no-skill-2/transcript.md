**The design.** One token contract, byte-identical and at the same address on both chains, exposing only a role-gated ERC-7802 `crosschainMint`/`crosschainBurn` pair. Burn-then-mint against a single global cap — no lockbox, no canonical home chain. The *transport* is just whatever holds `BRIDGE_ROLE`.

**Who carries the message.** Q4: LayerZero OFT (with CCIP/CCT as the pre-validated alternate), configured 2-of-N with at least one DVN independent of LayerZero Labs. **The split.** Commit is the token + one transport + the in-game flow (session keys, sponsored gas *and* message fee, in-transit UI, Retry via permissionless manual execution) + supply-invariant monitoring. The bet is Superchain native interop between the two chains — if it lands, it's a timelocked role grant on the `SuperchainTokenBridge` predeploy, not a migration.

Two things in there that are worth your attention specifically:

- **The deployment shape is a one-way door.** Same-address CREATE2 hashes init code, and constructor args are part of init code. If Base gets an `initialSupply` constructor arg and OP gets `0`, the addresses diverge and the interop option is permanently gone. Empty constructor, `initialize()` after, genesis mint on Base separately.
- **`BRIDGE_ROLE` can't be a boolean.** Once two bridges are authorized, compromise of either is an unbounded mint. Each bridge needs a rolling-window mint cap in the token itself.

On assumptions: the load-bearing one is EVM-equivalence + a deterministic deployer on both, and it expires the day you deploy — which is the argument for getting the deploy shape right in Q4 even if you ship the simplest transport. Base leaving the Superchain kills the bet but leaves the commit untouched, by construction.

One thing I flagged rather than decided: cutting the L1 leg means tokens burned toward a permanently dead chain have no canonical recovery route — only a timelocked treasury re-mint. That's a governance promise, not a protocol guarantee, and it's your call whether to say it out loud.
