Written to `answer.md`. The short version:

**The design.** Identical token on both chains, same address, ERC-20 + ERC-7802 (`crosschainMint`/`crosschainBurn`) + an xERC20-style registry of allowed bridges with per-bridge rate limits. Moving a balance is burn-on-source, mint-on-destination. Mint authority for *new* supply exists on one chain only, so the cross-chain sum can't inflate unless a registered adapter lies — and the rate limit bounds that. The token itself knows nothing about bridges; messaging lives in swappable adapters.

**Who carries the message.** Superchain interop is what you actually want — same trust as the chains themselves, gas-only cost, ~block latency — but mainnet enablement between those two specific chains is someone else's schedule, so I'd ship on a third-party adapter (LayerZero, as an adapter rather than making the token an OFT) and keep the exit. Ruled out the canonical L1 bridge: it's L2→L1→L2 and the ~7-day withdrawal window is unshippable for a game.

**On "no relayer."** There's always a relayer; the question is whose. The third-party executor means zero infra from you, and the thing that makes that hold in the failure case is that retry must be permissionless — the player's own wallet can finish a stuck transfer. Your ops burden becomes a monitor, not a service.

**Commit vs. bet.** Commit: contracts, one live route both directions, in-game one-signature flow with an honest "in transit" state, gas drop on arrival, guardian pause, supply-invariant alerting, audit. Bet: interop as primary route, instant-feel fast fill (that's a liquidity business, not a Q4 line item), fully gasless moves, a third chain.

**The assumption most worth your attention:** neither chain reorgs in practice. Fast burn/mint between them is only safe because both run a single sequencer today. If either moves to decentralized or shared sequencing, confirmation depths need revisiting *before* that upgrade lands. I'd put a named owner on watching for it.

Three open questions at the end that would firm up the rate-limit posture and the fee math — which chain mints, whether the token is tradable or closed-loop, and expected moves/day.
