Payment flow in detail\n\n**Why the caller needs no ETH.** EIP-3009 splits authorization from execution. The caller signs an\nEIP-712 `TransferWithAuthorization` struct — `from`, `to`, `value`, `validAfter`, `validBefore`,\n`nonce` — which is pure signing, no chain interaction, no gas. The facilitator submits\n`USDC.transferWithAuthorization(...)` and pays the ETH. USDC on Base implements EIP-3009 natively,\nso no wrapper contract or approval is needed. The `nonce` is a random 32-byte value consumed\nonchain by USDC, so replay is prevented at the token contract, not by us.\n\n**Request sequence in `server.ts`:**\n\n1. No payment header → `402` with `accepts[]` (scheme `exact`, network `eip155:8453`, token USDC,\n   amount `\"350000\"`, `payTo`). `350000` atomic units = $0.35 at 6 decimals.\n2. Payment header present → **local pre-checks first** (scheme, network, token, `payTo`, amount ≥\n   price, `validBefore` at least 30s out, nonce not already used). Rejecting malformed payloads\n   locally keeps facilitator round-trips off the abuse path.\n3. `POST {facilitator}/verify` — signature validity, payer balance, deadline. Invalid → `402` again\n   with the reason, so the client can correct and retry rather than guess.\n4. **Do the work** (upstream fetch). Fails → `502`, nothing settled, caller not charged.\n5. `POST {facilitator}/settle` — the USDC transfer lands on Base. Fails → `502`, no data returned.\n6. `200` with the forecast, `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` (tx hash, payer, network), and the feedback receipt.\n\n**Ordering trade-off.** Verify → work → settle is the x402 convention, and it's right here: verify\nalready proves the signature and balance are good, so settlement failing after we've done the work\nis rare, and the work is one cheap upstream call. The alternative (settle first) would mean\nrefunding on upstream failure — which needs ETH, an onchain write, and a refund policy. We take\nthe small revenue-leak risk instead of building a refund path.\n\n**Idempotency.** Settled nonces are cached with their responses (TTL 10 min). A client that retries\nafter a dropped connection gets the same forecast, not a second charge. In-memory today; Redis if\nthis runs multi-instance — **noted as a real limitation: with more than one instance and no shared\nstore, a retry can hit a cold instance and be charged twice.**\n\n**Pricing** is a flat `exact` $0.35. The `upto` scheme exists for metered work, but our unit of\nwork is fixed-size, so `exact` is correct and simpler for the client to reason about.\n\n---\n\n## 8. Reputation flow in detail\n\nThe problem with open feedback: anyone can spam ratings for an agent they never called. ERC-8004\naddresses this by having readers filter to a `trustedClients` set, but that pushes the work onto\nthe reader. We make ratings *verifiable* instead:\n\nOn a successful call, the response includes a **receipt** — `agentId`, payer address, settlement\ntx hash, amount, endpoint, timestamp — plus its `keccak256` hash and an **EIP-712 signature over\nit from our attestation key**.\n\nThe caller then posts:\n\n```solidity\nreputationRegistry.giveFeedback(\n  agentId,              // 42\n  92, 0,                // value, valueDecimals  → \"92/100\"\n  \"quality\", \"forecast\",// tag1, tag2\n  \"https://forecast.example.com/forecast\",\n  \"ipfs://<receipt-CID>\",   // optional: the receipt JSON, pinned by the client\n  receiptHash               // bytes32 — binds this rating to a real settled payment\n);\n```\n\nAny prospective caller can then take the `receiptHash` from a feedback entry, fetch the receipt,\ncheck our signature, and confirm the tx hash is a real USDC transfer to our `payTo` on Base. A\nrating with no matching receipt is discountable as noise. This costs us nothing to offer and makes\nour reputation harder to forge *in either direction* — we can't manufacture praise either, since\nwe can't manufacture payments to ourselves without paying the fees.\n\nWe do not gate `giveFeedback`; a caller can rate us badly and we cannot stop them. That is the\npoint. Reading is `getSummary(agentId, trustedClients, tag1, tag2)` and needs nothing from us.\n\n**Limitation, stated plainly:** the receipt proves *a paid call happened*, not that the forecast\nwas accurate. Verifying forecast quality is what the ERC-8004 Validation Registry is for\n(crypto-economic or TEE-backed validators scoring 0–100), and we don't participate in it. Our\nregistration JSON says `supportedTrust: [\"reputation\"]` for exactly that reason.\n\n---\n\n## 9.
