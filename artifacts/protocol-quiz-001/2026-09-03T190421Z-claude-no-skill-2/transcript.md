Written to `answer.md`. The short version:

**Build on today's MPT proofs (`eth_getProof` shape), behind a swappable scheme interface. Don't build on Verkle.**

**Where the state layer is.** Mainnet is still a hexary keccak MPT: account trie under the header's `stateRoot`, storage trie under each account's `storageRoot`. Nothing in 2026 changes that.

**Verkle is dead.** EIP-6800 lost its slot — its vector commitments aren't post-quantum safe, and STARK proving improvements erased the proof-size argument that justified them. No fork slot, no active client push. Worth flagging: `ethereum.org/roadmap/verkle-trees` is stale and still presents Verkle as the plan, so it's an easy trap.

**Where it's genuinely going.** EIP-7864, a unified binary tree — 4x shorter branches, and account+storage merged into one tree, so `storageRoot` disappears as a proof anchor and your two-step verification collapses to one. You can't implement it yet regardless: the EIP explicitly says the hash function is undecided (BLAKE3 is a placeholder, Poseidon2 pending cryptanalysis), so the encodings aren't frozen.

**Timing — two things that shape how hard a dependency you can take:**

1. EIP-7864 has missed two consecutive scoping rounds. Glamsterdam (Q4 2026) is ePBS + BALs; Hegotá's headliner is FOCIL. 2027 at the earliest, and slippage is the base rate here. Worse for planning: 7864 is an *overlay* — the binary tree starts empty, the MPT is frozen but alive, and bulk migration is a separate later fork (EIP-7748, no slot). So for an extended period a slot lives in one tree or the other and you must verify against both. That makes *proven absence* in the binary tree security-critical, since absence is what authorizes the MPT fallback.

2. **The change that hits you first isn't the trie — it's ePBS.** Under EIP-7732, `ExecutionPayloadHeader` becomes `ExecutionPayloadBid` and drops `state_root` entirely, keeping only `block_hash`. Today you read `stateRoot` straight out of the beacon block; afterward you must fetch the full execution header and check `keccak(rlp(header)) == block_hash`. Still trust-minimized, but it's real work landing ~a year before anything touches the trie. Budget for it in 2026.

The recommendation leans on a point worth stating directly: the trie format isn't where your trust-minimization lives. An MPT proof is self-verifying, so a hostile RPC can only lie about *which* `stateRoot` is current. Spend your effort on running the sync-committee light client yourself, and document that the residual trust is the sync-committee assumption — weaker than a full node.

I also flagged the L1 zkEVM path as track-don't-build: the `ExecutionWitness` format is still being standardized and the target consumer is stateless validators, not third-party light clients.
