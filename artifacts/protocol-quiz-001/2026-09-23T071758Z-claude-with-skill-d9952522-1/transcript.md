Written to `answer.md`. The short version:

**Build on today's MPT proofs (`eth_getProof` / EIP-1186), and get your trust-minimization from the consensus layer, not the tree format.**

What I verified against live sources (not memory):

- **Verkle is the thing to avoid.** EIP-6800 never shipped, no client completed migration, and research pivoted off it during 2025–2026 — it needs a trusted setup and isn't post-quantum. Caveat: ethereum.org's roadmap page *still* presents Verkle as the live path. That page is stale; don't let it mislead your team.
- **Binary tree (EIP-7864) is the genuine direction, but it is unscheduled.** Status is **Draft** with **no fork relationship** — not SFI, not CFI, absent from Glamsterdam and absent from the EF's 62-EIP Hegotá tier list published Sep 7. Neither fork has a state-tree headliner.
- **The hash function isn't decided.** The EIP text explicitly says BLAKE3 in the reference impl is not final, with Keccak and Poseidon2 still candidates. That alone makes a hard dependency unsafe — it's the primitive your verifier implements.
- **Timing:** Glamsterdam is mid-testnet (Sepolia proposed Sep 28, Hoodi Oct 26), so mainnet late 2026/early 2027; Hegotá follows with FOCIL. State tree is at least two forks out, realistically 2027+ — and that's my inference from cadence, not anyone's commitment.

The architectural point I'd emphasize most: the state-tree format is the *least* important part of your trust-minimization. Following the sync committee to a beacon header, then SSZ-proving down to the execution payload's `stateRoot`, is what actually removes the RPC provider from your trust set — and that layer is identical under every proposal on the table. Only the final slot-proof step changes. Build so that's the one replaceable piece.

Two things in the doc that are easy to miss and will bite: your verifier must handle MPT **exclusion** proofs (absent slot = a valid `0`, not an error), and you should decide explicitly whether "recent block" means optimistic head (~1 slot, reorg-able) or finalized (~13 min) — that's a bigger correctness question for you than the tree format is.
