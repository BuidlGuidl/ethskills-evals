# Two-Tranche Yield Vault Design

## Overview

The vault runs on Arbitrum and accepts a common deposit asset, assumed to be a stablecoin such as USDC unless a later implementation chooses another accounting asset. At deposit time, each user selects one of two tranches:

- **Tranche A:** fixed-rate exposure with a maturity date. The user receives a rate locked from the market price available at deposit time.
- **Tranche B:** variable, higher-risk exposure to fees paid by leveraged traders on Arbitrum.

The vault should track each tranche separately. Tranche A needs deposit-time accounting because users entering on different days may lock different fixed rates for the same maturity. Tranche B can use standard share accounting because returns are variable and pro rata.

## Tranche A: Fixed Rate via Pendle PT

Tranche A deploys into **Pendle Principal Tokens (PTs)** on Arbitrum.

Pendle splits a yield-bearing asset into:

- **SY:** the standardized wrapper around the yield-bearing asset.
- **PT:** principal redeemable at maturity.
- **YT:** the yield stream until maturity.

For Tranche A, the vault buys PT for the selected maturity. PT normally trades at a discount to its redemption value. That discount is the fixed yield. When a user deposits, the vault quotes the fixed rate from the actual PT purchase price after fees and slippage, then records that rate and maturity for the user's position.

### How Tranche A Earns

Tranche A earns from PT accretion:

1. The user deposits USDC into Tranche A.
2. The vault swaps or routes the deposit into the chosen Pendle PT market for the target maturity.
3. The vault records the PT amount purchased and the implied fixed rate at execution.
4. At maturity, PT is redeemable for the underlying standardized yield asset at par.
5. The vault converts the redeemed asset back to the accounting asset if needed and pays the user the fixed maturity amount.

This is economically similar to buying a zero-coupon bond. The return is not generated by active trading; it is locked in by purchasing principal at a discount and holding it to maturity.

### Keeper Role for Tranche A

The keeper does not need to harvest continuous yield from the PT itself. Its main jobs are operational:

- execute queued deposits into PT markets;
- reinvest dust, rewards, or idle balances according to vault policy;
- monitor maturity and redeem matured PT;
- roll matured or unclaimed balances into a new maturity only if the user or product rules allow it.

The implementation should avoid promising compound interest for Tranche A unless the strategy explicitly reinvests interim rewards or supports maturity rolling. The fixed rate should come from the PT purchase price, not from keeper discretion.

### Tranche A Risks

- **Pendle smart contract risk:** a bug or exploit in Pendle, the PT market, or the SY wrapper can impair funds.
- **Underlying asset risk:** if the yield-bearing asset behind the PT depegs, is hacked, or fails to redeem as expected, the PT may not be money-good at maturity.
- **Liquidity and slippage risk:** PT markets can be thin, especially far from popular maturities. Large deposits may lock worse rates than quoted.
- **Maturity mismatch risk:** users expect payment at a specific maturity. The vault must not place funds into the wrong PT maturity or an asset with a different redemption profile.
- **Early exit risk:** if the product supports withdrawals before maturity, users may receive the market value of PT, which can be below the promised maturity value.
- **Oracle and pricing risk:** deposit-time rate calculations must use execution results or robust market pricing, not stale spot estimates.
- **Keeper risk:** failure to redeem at maturity or execute deposits promptly can leave assets idle or expose users to rate drift.

## Tranche B: Trader-Fee Yield via GMX V2 GM Pools

Tranche B deploys into **GMX V2 GM pools** on Arbitrum.

GMX V2 uses isolated liquidity pools for each market. Liquidity providers deposit the market's backing assets and receive GM tokens representing their pool share. Traders use those pools for leveraged long and short positions.

Preferred Tranche B markets should be highly liquid, fully backed markets such as ETH/USD or BTC/USD rather than long-tail synthetic markets. The exact markets should be governed by risk limits, utilization, available liquidity, and vault asset compatibility.

### How Tranche B Earns

Tranche B earns variable yield from fees paid by GMX traders and users, including:

- trading fees from opening and closing leveraged positions;
- borrowing fees paid by leveraged traders;
- liquidation fees when unhealthy positions are liquidated;
- swap fees from pool rebalancing and user swaps;
- any protocol incentives distributed to GM liquidity providers.

The return is not fixed. It depends on trader activity, market volatility, pool utilization, and trader profitability.

### Keeper Role for Tranche B

The keeper compounds Tranche B by:

- claiming GMX rewards and incentives when available;
- swapping rewards into the correct pool backing assets;
- adding liquidity back into selected GM pools;
- rebalancing across approved GM markets if allocations drift outside risk limits;
- enforcing caps when utilization, skew, or open interest makes a market too risky.

Keeper logic should be bounded by onchain parameters so it cannot chase yield into unapproved markets or exceed concentration limits.

### Tranche B Risks

- **Trader PnL risk:** GM liquidity providers are the counterparty to traders. If traders are profitable, the pool can lose value.
- **Market and inventory risk:** GM pools hold volatile backing assets such as ETH, BTC, and stablecoins. Pool value changes with asset prices and pool composition.
- **Utilization and open-interest risk:** high leverage demand can increase fees but also increase exposure to large trader wins or rapid pool imbalance.
- **Synthetic-market risk:** synthetic or long-tail markets can require auto-deleveraging or other protective mechanisms under stress. The vault should prefer fully backed markets unless governance explicitly accepts this risk.
- **Oracle risk:** GMX depends on price feeds for positions, liquidations, and pool accounting. Bad or delayed prices can transfer value.
- **Liquidity risk:** exiting GM positions during market stress may be slow, expensive, or subject to unfavorable pool pricing.
- **Smart contract risk:** GMX contracts, reward routers, and integrations can fail or be exploited.
- **Keeper and compounding risk:** missed harvests reduce realized APY, while bad swaps or rebalances can leak value.

## Shared Vault Controls

The vault should include shared controls before implementation:

- separate accounting for Tranche A and Tranche B assets, shares, and liabilities;
- per-maturity accounting for Tranche A fixed-rate deposits;
- approved Pendle markets and GMX markets configured by governance;
- deposit caps per tranche, maturity, market, and underlying asset;
- slippage limits for PT purchases, reward swaps, and GM liquidity operations;
- emergency pause for deposits and strategy actions;
- withdrawal rules that clearly distinguish maturity redemption from early exit;
- keeper permissions limited to harvest, compound, redeem, and rebalance within configured bounds.

## Summary

Tranche A should use Pendle PTs because they directly express fixed-rate, maturity-based yield on Arbitrum. Its yield is the discount between PT purchase price and maturity redemption value.

Tranche B should use GMX V2 GM pools because they expose the vault to fees generated by leveraged trading activity on Arbitrum. Its yield is variable and comes from trading, borrowing, liquidation, swap, and incentive flows, with LPs accepting trader PnL and market risk in exchange.
