**The framing needs to change before this goes to finance.** Almost everything published about Ethereum state relief — state expiry, statelessness, Verkle/binary trees — reduces what a *validating* node must store. An archive node's job is to retain exactly what those proposals discard. Relief for the network is not relief for your fleet.

What I confirmed:

- **Live, banked:** partial history expiry (shipped Jul 2025, 300–500 GB one-time). Worth verifying every node actually took it.
- **SFI, Glamsterdam, mainnet 2026-11-04** (ACD-confirmed, final devnet phase): EIP-8037 reprices state creation hard — new storage slot 20,000 → 97,920 gas, new account 25,000 → 183,600. But it was finalized *specifically to make a gas-limit increase safe*. You grow ~116 GiB/yr at today's 60M limit; EIP-8037's worst-case bound at 200M is 160 GiB/yr. **The absolute growth rate is permitted to rise.** What improves is growth per unit of economic activity.
- **Hegotá (EIP-8081):** exactly two EIPs scheduled — FOCIL and Frame Transaction. Nothing state-related is even *Considered*. Its May 2027 date is explicitly a projection, not a commitment.
- **Zero fork relationship:** EIP-7864 binary tree (Draft, not in either fork's scope — not even Hegotá's ~50-item wish list; hash function still undecided), state expiry (EIP-7736 is Stagnant *and* written against Verkle, an abandoned design), rolling-window history expiry, statelessness. Verkle being dropped after years as the consensus answer is itself the cautionary tale.

So the brief tells finance to assume **no protocol relief for archive nodes in the window** and books savings that are purely operational: migrating any legacy hash-based Geth archive nodes (12 TB+ → ~2 TB on Erigon 3.x — the largest immediate win, available today), tiering the fleet so recent-state queries stop hitting archive nodes, and moving analytics workloads to columnar extracts. The EF's own position is that out-of-protocol archive work is forward-compatible, so none of it is wasted.

Two things I'd flag as needing your attention on a date, not a budget cycle: the **gas-limit votes after Glamsterdam** drive your model more than any EIP and aren't on any fork calendar; and the **repricing breakage audit** (§3.6) has a hard 2026-11-04 deadline — hardcoded 21,000 intrinsic gas, deployment estimates going ~4.9M → ~37.8M, and any internal simulator assuming one-dimensional gas.
